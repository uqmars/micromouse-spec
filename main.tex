\section{Introduction}
\vspace*{9mm}
Micromouse is a robotics competition which has been run across the world since the 1970`s. In it, competitors are tasked with developing a small autonomous robotic \textit{mouse} that finds the quickest path to the centre of an $N\times N$ maze. In addition to this core challenge, there is an additional challenge for passing and avoiding \emph{gates}.

\subsection{Important Details}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Each competitor will need to pay $\$5$AUD to enter.
    \item Teams can calibrate their mouse on an example maze on the $\textrm{7}^\textrm{th}$ of December 2023.
    \item The competition will be held on the $\textrm{8}^\textrm{th}$ of December 2023.
    \item Team entries close on the \textcolor{red}{$\textrm{24}^\textrm{th}$ of November 2023} at 5pm AEST. Fees will be charged the following week.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Competition Rules}
\subsection{Team Eligibility}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item All members of the team must fall into one of the following current enrollments:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item Master`s student.
        \item Undergraduate University Student.
        \item High School Student, provided they are accompanied by a teacher.
    \end{enumerate}
    \item Teams may have a maximum of five official members.
    \item Teams must register using the \href{https://forms.gle/P9X8jZgmrGdG6HyK7}{this link} and provide the following supporting evidence in their GitHub Repo:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item The source code for their micromouse.
        \item A design document in the form of a pdf.
        \item A 5min video of the team discussing their design decisions made for the micromouse.
    \end{enumerate}
    \item One mentor is allowed per team but cannot have significant input to the design of the micromouse.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Mircomouse Specifications}


\begin{enumerate}
    \item The total budget for the micromouse must not exceed $\$1,500$AUD. 
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item A list of components and their costs must be submitted with a copy of receipts must be sent with other documentation.
    \end{enumerate}
    \item The length and width of the mouse must both be under $16$cm.
    \item The height of the mouse must not exceed $8$cm.
    \item The mouse can only be powered via battery.
    \item The mouse must be controlled via on-board system. External connection by any-means during a teams round will result in disqualification.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Maze Specification}
\begin{center}
    \begin{figure}[H]
    \caption{Maze Wall Specification}\label{fig:maze-spec}
        \begin{center}
            \includegraphics[scale=0.5]{Assets/Maze-spec.png}
        \end{center}
    \end{figure}
\end{center}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item The maze will be made up of a $9\times 9$ unit grid, where each unit length will be $18$cm approx.
    \item The maze will be comprised of wood.
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item The walls and floor of the maze will be painted white.
        \item Time altering sections will have RFID tags with specific hex codes in the centre of of the cell.
        \item The maze will be $10$cm tall and $1.2$cm thick.
        \item Passageways will be 16.8cm wide.
    \end{enumerate}
    \item The outside of the maze will be surrounded by a wall.
    \item The mouse will start at one of four corners of the maze. 
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item When placed at the starting point, the outside walls will be to the left and behind the robot.
    \end{enumerate}
    \item Maze dimensions will be accurate to $5\%$ or $2$mm, whichever is less.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Challenge Rules}
\vspace*{12mm}
\begin{center}
    \emph{Rules are subject to change with notice provided to given emails.}
\end{center}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item No modifications may be made to the navigation algorithm of the mouse following the reveal of the maze on competition day.
    \item For each team's turn, they will be given three minutes with their mouse to make any adjustments/sensor calibrations. No details of the maze or its configuration should be entered into the mouse.
    \item Teams will be allocated a window of 10 minutes to compete. This will begin with a three-minute calibration time. The micromouse will then be given seven-minutes to solve the maze\label{rule:time-limit}.
    \item Teams may choose to end their calibration period early, but any remaining time will not be added onto the seven-minute window.
    \item You may adjust the mouse between runs but this will be included in the time used in a teams seven-minute window.
    \item A team's micromouse is allow up to 7 runs.
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item The quickest of these runs will be considered.
    \end{enumerate}
    \item The run time (time to reach the centre) will start when the mouse has left its starting square and stop when it finds the centre.
    \item No modifications should be made between runs which drastically alters the mouse's weight or profile during run time, such as removing sensor arrays or switching to lighter batteries.
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item Teams may be allowed to change batteries, adjust sensors, and make repairs between runs.
    \end{enumerate}
    \item The mouse may continue to map the maze once it has found the centre.
    \item Do not make any assumptions about the lighting level in the room.
    \item Nothing may be discarded by the robot (or leave a trail). This will lead to a disqualification.
    \item The mouse may not climb or fly over the walls of the maze.
    \item Any attempt to destroy walls/parts of the maze by either teams or the robot will lead to a disqualification.
    \item The team's mouse must be robotic. The use of live mice or rats will result in disqualification.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Prizes}

\subsection{Quickest Time Solving the $9\times 9$ Maze}
The micromouse which is capable of finding the centre of the maze at the lowest time will be the winner of this challenge.

\vspace*{-10mm}

\subsection{Cheapest Micromouse Design}
Though the budget is limited to $\$1,500$AUD, competitors will be awarded for designing the cheapest micromouse which is capable of finding the centre, so long as it is under the time given in \textit{Challenge Rules} \ref{rule:time-limit}.

\vspace*{-10mm}

\subsection{Passing and Avoiding Gates}
The maze given for this challenge will be of the same $9\times 9$ grid as the \textit{Quickest Time Solving an $n\times n$ Maze} but with the addition of \textit{gates} to both prioritise and avoid. Figure \ref{fig:gated-maze} represents a maze with gates added; green to pass through and red to avoid. These gates are RFID tags with specific hex codes for each type. The hex code will be provided to the teams prior to the maze reveal. 

\emph{Note: The ground will not be coloured. The depiction in Figure \ref{fig:gated-maze} is for conceptualisation only.}

\begin{center}
    \begin{figure}[H]
    \caption{Potential $9\times 9$ Maze with Added Gates}\label{fig:gated-maze}
        \begin{center}
            \includegraphics[scale=0.35]{Assets/Gated-Maze.png}
        \end{center}
    \end{figure}
\end{center}

This challenge has the following rules:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item If a mouse passes through a red gate five-seconds will be added to their time.
    \item If a mouse passes through a green gate three-seconds will be deducted from their time.
    \item The mouse with the fastest time with the gates accounted for will be the winner.
\end{enumerate}
